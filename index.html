Nine to Thrive Privacy Policy

Last updated: 21 October 2026

The short version

Everything stays on your phone. There is no account, no ads, no analytics and no tracking. The app has no internet permission, so it cannot send your data anywhere. We (the makers) never see your data.

What the app keeps on your phone

The app saves these things in its own private storage on your phone only:

  • Your nickname and, if you enter it, your weight (used only to suggest a water goal).
  • The places you set: your office, an optional home office and an optional home address.
  • When you arrived at and left those places on workdays.
  • Your breaks, glasses of water and step counts. Steps are counted all day, from when your wake-up alarm is turned off until your bedtime in Settings.
  • The time of your next wake-up alarm, read from the phone's clock app. It is used only to know when your day starts counting steps.
  • Your focus sprints and micro-reset taps (when they happened).
  • Energy ratings, tasks, "park list" items and notes you type in the app.
  • Your settings, and any sound file you choose for reminders or sleep.

Other apps cannot read this storage. The app turns off Android's cloud backup, so this data is not copied to Google Drive or anywhere else. It is also not moved when you transfer to a new phone. To keep a copy, use "Export my data (CSV)" in Settings.

On your home screen and lock screen

Widgets you choose to place can show your tasks, your note, water and breaks. Reminders appear as notifications. Anyone who can see your screen can see them. You choose which widgets to place, and Android's lock-screen notification setting decides what shows on the lock screen.

Why the app asks for each permission

  • Location, including "Allow all the time": to notice when you arrive at and leave work, so reminders come only while you are there. The app uses Android's geofencing (an alert when you enter or leave an area). It does not track your route or keep a location history. Before bedtime it checks once whether you are at home.
  • Physical activity: to count your steps with the phone's step sensor.
  • Calendar (read only): to see if a meeting is happening right now, so a reminder can wait. Nothing from your calendar is saved.
  • Notifications: to show your reminders.
  • Alarms & reminders: so reminders arrive on time.
  • Run at startup: so your reminders come back after the phone restarts.
  • Media playback and foreground service: to keep playing a sleep melody with the screen off.
  • Network state and keep awake: added automatically by Android's background task tool (WorkManager), which the app uses for its hourly check. The app does not use them to go online.
  • An internal permission: only this app can use it (added automatically by Android's support library). It gives no access to anything.

You can say no to any permission. The app still works, with fewer features.

Google services on your phone

Two features use services built into Android by Google, which run outside the app:

  • Location detection (geofencing and the "I'm here now" button) uses Google Play services location.
  • Address search uses Android's address lookup. The address you type is looked up by that service, which may use the internet.

These are handled by Google under Google's Privacy Policy. The app itself sends nothing to Google or anyone else.

Guided breathing uses your phone's own text-to-speech voice. The app only passes it the words to speak.

You are in control

  • Export my data (CSV): in Settings. You choose where the file is saved. The app does not send it anywhere. The file has one row per workday: date, times you arrived and left, minutes at work, break minutes, breaks taken, water, steps at work, daily win, energy rating, parked items, micro-resets and tasks done.
  • Delete all data: in Settings. It removes everything the app has stored.
  • Uninstall: removing the app also removes all its data.

Children

The app is made for adults at work. It is not aimed at children under 13.

Health note

Water, break and step suggestions are general habits, not medical advice.
